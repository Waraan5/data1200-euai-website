Understanding the EU AI Act Risk Categories

One of the most important concepts in the EU AI Act is the idea of risk-based regulation.

The European Union recognizes that not all AI systems pose the same level of risk.

An AI tool that recommends movies on a streaming platform does not create the same concerns as an AI system used to evaluate job applicants, approve loans or support medical decisions.

Because of this, the EU AI Act does not apply the same rules to every AI system.

At its core, the EU AI Act follows a simple principle:

The higher the potential impact on individuals, society or fundamental rights, the stricter the requirements become.

The Four Risk Categories

The EU AI Act divides AI systems into four main categories:

  • Unacceptable Risk
  • High Risk
  • Limited Risk
  • Minimal Risk

Understanding these categories is essential because they determine what organizations can and cannot do when developing or using AI.

🔴 Unacceptable Risk

At the highest level are AI systems that are considered an unacceptable threat to people and society.

These systems are prohibited because the risks are deemed too significant to justify their use.

Examples

  • Manipulating human behaviour in harmful ways
  • Exploiting vulnerable individuals
  • Social scoring systems
  • Certain forms of mass biometric surveillance
  • Predicting criminal behaviour solely through profiling

🟠 High Risk

High-Risk AI systems are not banned, but they are subject to strict requirements due to their potential impact on people's lives.

Examples

  • Recruitment and hiring
  • Healthcare and medical diagnostics
  • Education
  • Credit scoring
  • Critical infrastructure
  • Border management
  • Law enforcement

Required Safeguards

Organizations deploying High-Risk AI must implement:

  • Risk assessments
  • Human oversight
  • Data quality controls
  • Documentation and record-keeping
  • Security measures
  • Ongoing monitoring and testing

🟡 Limited Risk

Limited Risk systems generally present lower risks but require transparency.

People should know when they are interacting with AI.

Examples

  • Customer service chatbots
  • Virtual assistants
  • AI-generated images
  • AI-generated text
  • Deepfakes

🟢 Minimal Risk

Most AI applications belong to this category.

Examples

  • Spam filters
  • Search functions
  • Recommendation engines
  • Inventory systems
  • Video game AI
  • Productivity tools

Why Does the Risk-Based Approach Matter?

A movie recommendation engine and an AI system used to screen job applicants are not equivalent.

One affects entertainment choices. The other can affect careers, income and future opportunities.

By focusing regulatory efforts on higher-risk applications, the EU AI Act seeks to protect citizens while avoiding unnecessary barriers to innovation.

A Simple Way to Think About It

🔴 Unacceptable Risk → Prohibited

🟠 High Risk → Allowed with strict requirements

🟡 Limited Risk → Allowed with transparency obligations

🟢 Minimal Risk → Few or no obligations
